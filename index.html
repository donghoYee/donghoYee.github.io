Research

Rosota · first author · ICRA 2027 submission

Learning laparoscopic appendectomy from surgeons' own instruments

Most minimally invasive surgery is still done by hand, and the surgeon's instrument motion is lost when the operation ends. We record it instead, with a small logger clipped to the surgeon's ordinary instrument, and train a surgical robot policy on nothing else.

  • SurgiLogger recovers roll, depression, insertion depth and jaw opening from an IMU, a time-of-flight sensor and a Hall sensor; 0.17–0.42° attitude and 1.36 mm depth RMS against a Franka FR3 ground truth.
  • Latency matching. Each sensor channel reaches the log with its own delay; rewinding each by its measured latency raised closed-loop lift success from 19% to 81%.
  • Closed-loop policy selection. 31 candidate configurations were run in an Isaac Sim scene reconstructed from depth maps of real organs; a frozen DINOv3 backbone cut trocar violations from 88% to 6% relative to ResNet-18.
  • In vivo. Retrained on 849 demonstrations from four live rabbits, the diffusion policy completed the appendectomy with electrosurgery in three of four further animals under the surgeon's supervision (peak RCM error 0.84 mm; all animals healthy at seven days). To our knowledge the first learned policy to fire electrosurgery in a live animal.

Rosota · device and data

SurgiLogger

I designed the device end to end: mechanics, firmware, the BLE link, calibration against the robot, and the offline alignment that turns raw streams into observation–action pairs. Nothing is added to the part of the instrument that enters the patient, so surgeons work as usual and the logger runs a full operating-room day on one charge. The current version adds a strain gauge that senses the interaction force between the instrument tip and tissue, so that policies can learn how hard a surgeon pulls, not only where the instrument goes.

The company's first patent application, Detachable Surgical Data Recording Apparatus for Manual Laparoscopic Surgical Instrument (KR 10-2026-0150277, 14 claims), and its first design application (KR 30-2026-0037733) name me as lead inventor. The two demonstration corpora recorded with it, 533 ex-vivo and 849 in-vivo episodes, are released with the paper.

Next: the first human cases at Seoul National University Hospital (IRB under review, PI Prof. Kyu Eun Lee), and force-aware policies trained on the new channel.

Rosota · co-first author · ICRA 2027 submission

Tool-tip localization through the drape with UWB carrier phase

In the operating room an instrument's absolute position cannot be read from its own sensors: the trocar's location is unknown to them, the sterile drape blocks every optical path, and the metal in the field disturbs electromagnetic trackers. What is left is time-of-flight ranging, which scatters by centimeters. We built a ranging sensor accurate to a millimeter instead: an ultra-wideband node clipped near the handle of each instrument and one on the endoscope, all outside the sterile barrier, ranging to one another by carrier phase at σ = 0.10–0.33 mm and 35–46 Hz among metal instruments.

Three distances cannot locate two free-floating instruments, but each shaft pivots at its trocar, so its antenna keeps a single degree of freedom. Two unknowns now face three measurements; the spare equation exposes a wrong integer or a slipped mount instead of absorbing it, and the same solve returns both tool tips in the endoscope frame. The logger was carried into a live rabbit appendectomy.

Rosota · platform and team

The platform behind six ICRA 2027 submissions

I lead Rosota's device, instrument and surgical-robot (control & policy) teams. The dual-FR3 laparoscopic platform with its 1 kHz remote-center-of-motion controller, the imitation-learning stack, and the simulation protocol are shared across the lab's work, and I am a co-author on all six papers the team submitted to ICRA 2027 between June and September 2026:

  • a magnetometer-free instrument-state logger fusing inertial, range and visual sensing (1.21 mm RMS against the robot);
  • a surgical master console built from two general-purpose 6-DoF arms, validated in a porcine cholecystectomy;
  • a contact-stable deformable-tissue simulator reconstructed from a single RGB-D recording;
  • contact-adaptive robotic ultrasound from robot-free image–motion demonstrations.

Rosota, founded with surgeons and engineers from the MEDISC Lab, closed a seed round led by FuturePlay (with Schmidt and ZDVC, US$1.1M) in mid-2026 and was selected for Korea's TIPS R&D program (US$0.7M) in October 2026.

INRoL, Seoul National University · Prof. Dongjun Lee

Humanoid foundation models and tactile simulation

I joined INRoL in September 2025, just as NVIDIA released Isaac GR00T, and built a bridge between Isaac Sim and GR00T N1.5 for the Fourier GR1: fine-tuning and roll-out tutorials, pick-and-place and nut-pouring environments, an evaluation pipeline, and notes on what breaks. That "RFM Wiki" became the hands-on material of the graduate course Advanced Topics in Mechanical Design 1: AI Robotics and Robot Foundation Models (Prof. Yongseok Lee, now at DGIST), where I was the teaching assistant in Fall 2025.

My senior thesis, with Ph.D. student Somang Lee, is on tactile sensing for humanoid hands. I added a Pasternak-foundation tactile module to the lab's CRISP simulator so that stress propagates between neighboring taxels, which Winkler-type contact models cannot do, and benchmarked seven simulators against FEniCSx FEM on four indenters: peak-pressure error of 4–17% at about 0.5 ms per frame on a 60 × 60 grid. I am now identifying the elastomer, friction and per-taxel parameters of a commercial fingertip sensor from robot indentation data with a differentiable forward model, for CRISP and Isaac Sim/Newton.
